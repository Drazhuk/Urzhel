# Urzhel

> A glass jaw closes around the candle that casts tomorrow's shadow.

## The House Beneath the Quarry

The quarry workers found the house after removing a stone that had never warmed in sunlight. It stood upright beneath the hillside, its roof supporting neither soil nor darkness, but a narrow strip of sky filled with unmoving birds.

There were three rooms. The first contained a table set with empty dishes. The second contained a chair facing the wall. The third held a glass jaw suspended above a candle. No one found a staircase, although footsteps could be heard above each ceiling.

The foreman wrote down the number of rooms. By evening, his page showed four. He crossed out the final number and discovered the next morning that the candle had moved into the empty space beside his writing.

He returned the page to the house. The glass jaw opened slightly, and the birds above the roof changed their direction without moving their wings.

## A Candle With Two Wicks

The candle had two wicks, though only one was visible at a time. Lit from the left, it showed the room as it stood. Lit from the right, it showed a doorway in the wall behind the chair.

The workers tried touching that doorway. Their hands met stone. They tried covering the flame. Its light continued beneath their palms, revealing a latch made from a curved white tooth.

A woman from the village recognized the latch. She had dreamed of holding it while someone knocked on the other side. In every dream, she had waited for the knocking to stop before trying to open the door.

She brought a bowl of salt to the quarry. The foreman asked why. She said the dream's floor had always been wet, and she had grown tired of waking with the taste of seawater in her mouth.

## What the Chair Had Heard

The woman sat facing the wall. Beneath her, the chair began to creak in time with the footsteps overhead. The candle's second wick appeared, bent toward the glass jaw.

She held the salt beside the flame. Each grain acquired a tiny shadow. Together, the shadows showed a coast with a house standing at the edge of the water. In its window sat a person facing inward, holding the same bowl.

The woman remembered the knocking differently then. It had come from beneath the floor. The person beyond the door had been asking her to move the chair, not open the latch.

She rose. The chair remained warm. On its seat lay the impression of a folded wing, too large for the wood but somehow contained within it. The foreman heard one final footstep overhead and looked toward the missing fourth room.

## The Jaw's Former Owner

The house had been built inside the breath of a dragon whose bones lay around the quarry. Its jaw had become glass when it stopped speaking. Its tongue remained beneath the village, holding a sentence that nobody above ground could finish.

The quarry stones were the spaces between its teeth. Removing one revealed a room. Removing another revealed a memory of a room, and the workers had never learned to tell the difference before bringing their lamps inside.

The glass jaw preserved the last word the creature had almost spoken. The candle warmed that word from two directions. One wick belonged to what had happened. The other belonged to the shadow cast before it did.

The woman poured a line of salt beneath the chair. Behind the wall, a latch turned without being touched.

## A Sentence Divided by Flame

Marks appeared along the jaw's lower edge:

*Ur — zhel — ka — voth.*

The foreman copied them onto the page beside his crossed-out room count. The fourth room appeared in the candlelight, though its entrance remained no wider than the space between two written letters.

Inside stood a person with a folded wing over one shoulder. Their hand rested on a little door beneath the floor. Beyond it, something knocked three times, then waited.

The woman moved the chair across the salt line. The wing-shaped warmth on its seat became a small white feather. She placed the feather between the glass teeth.

The jaw closed. Both wicks burned at once. The coast within the salt shadows filled with ordinary daylight, and the person at its window set down the bowl.

## The Room Removed From the Account

The workers left before the stone above the house could settle. On the following morning, the quarry contained only bare rock. The cold stone that had covered the roof lay beside the foreman's tools, warm enough to dry his damp gloves.

His page listed three rooms. The crossed-out fourth number had become the drawing of a chair. He kept the page beneath a flat weight and never copied it again.

The woman returned home without her bowl. Her dreams still contained a door, but the knocking had ceased. Sometimes she heard a chair being moved carefully on the other side.

The quarry remains open. Birds cross its sky without stopping.

And in stone that no lamp has yet uncovered, a glass jaw holds one white feather between its teeth, casting a shadow that arrives a day before the light.
